RISK INVESTIGATION POLICY

PURPOSE

The risk engine prioritizes transactions for investigation based on machine learning evidence.

RISK LEVELS

HIGH RISK

Transactions with a final risk score of 0.79 or greater are classified as HIGH risk by the current project configuration.

HIGH-risk transactions should be prioritized for investigation.

The threshold is a project-defined operational threshold and should not be interpreted as a universal fraud threshold.

ALERT PRIORITY

CRITICAL alerts represent the highest investigation priority under the configured risk policy.

HIGH alerts represent transactions requiring significant investigation attention.

Risk priority should be determined using the configured risk engine rather than by manually changing individual transaction results.

INVESTIGATION REQUIREMENT

A high-risk alert should generate an investigation case containing:

- Transaction identifier.
- Timestep.
- Final risk score.
- XGBoost probability.
- GraphSAGE probability.
- Model agreement.
- Model disagreement.
- Evidence type.
- Graph evidence.
- SHAP evidence.
- Relevant retrieved investigation guidance.

MODEL CORROBORATION

When both XGBoost and GraphSAGE provide high-risk predictions, classify the evidence as stronger model corroboration.

When only one model provides strong evidence, the case should indicate the source of the stronger signal.

GRAPH EVIDENCE POLICY

High-risk neighbors strengthen graph-based evidence.

Low-risk neighbors do not prove that a transaction is legitimate.

Unknown neighbors should remain classified as unknown.

RECOMMENDED ACTIONS

For a high-risk transaction:

1. Escalate for manual review.
2. Review the model evidence.
3. Inspect graph relationships.
4. Review the highest-impact SHAP features.
5. Retrieve relevant investigation guidance.
6. Consider additional transaction context.
7. Record the investigation conclusion.

LIMITATIONS

The automated system supports investigators and does not replace human judgment.

A machine learning alert is not equivalent to confirmed fraud.

The final decision should consider additional evidence and applicable organizational policy.

The AI investigator must never fabricate transaction information, graph relationships, feature meanings, labels, or investigation evidence.
