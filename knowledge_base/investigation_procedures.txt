FRAUD INVESTIGATION PROCEDURES

PURPOSE

The purpose of investigation is to determine why a transaction was flagged, assess the strength of available evidence, and recommend an appropriate next action.

STEP 1 — REVIEW RISK SCORE

Start by reviewing the final risk score.

A high risk score indicates that the risk engine considers the transaction sufficiently suspicious to warrant investigation.

Risk score should be treated as a prioritization signal rather than proof of fraud.

STEP 2 — REVIEW MODEL EVIDENCE

Review the XGBoost and GraphSAGE probabilities.

Determine whether:

- Both models assign high risk.
- XGBoost assigns high risk while GraphSAGE assigns lower risk.
- GraphSAGE assigns high risk while XGBoost assigns lower risk.

Agreement between independent model perspectives strengthens the evidence.

Disagreement indicates that additional investigation may be useful.

STEP 3 — REVIEW SHAP EVIDENCE

Review the highest-impact SHAP features.

Focus on features with large positive SHAP values because they contributed toward the fraud prediction.

Do not invent semantic meanings for anonymous feature identifiers.

Use SHAP as evidence of model reasoning rather than as proof of fraud.

STEP 4 — REVIEW GRAPH EVIDENCE

Inspect the transaction's graph neighbors.

Review:

- Number of neighbors.
- Neighbor risk scores.
- Number of high-risk neighbors.
- High-risk neighbor rate.
- Known labels when available.

A high concentration of risky neighbors increases the strength of graph-based evidence.

Unknown neighbors should be treated as unknown rather than legitimate.

STEP 5 — ASSESS EVIDENCE STRENGTH

Strong corroboration occurs when:

- The final risk score is high.
- XGBoost risk is high.
- GraphSAGE risk is high.
- Model disagreement is small.
- Supporting graph evidence is present.

A strong transaction signal occurs when XGBoost is highly confident but GraphSAGE provides weaker evidence.

Graph-driven risk occurs when GraphSAGE is highly confident despite weaker XGBoost evidence.

STEP 6 — FORM INVESTIGATION CONCLUSION

The investigator should summarize:

1. Overall risk.
2. Model evidence.
3. Most important feature contributions.
4. Graph evidence.
5. Relevant fraud indicators.
6. Evidence limitations.

The conclusion should distinguish between model evidence and confirmed fraud.

STEP 7 — RECOMMEND ACTION

Recommended actions may include:

- Escalate for manual review.
- Request additional transaction context.
- Investigate connected transactions.
- Monitor the transaction or related activity.
- Hold or block only when required by an external policy.

The AI investigator should not claim that a transaction is definitively fraudulent based only on model output.
