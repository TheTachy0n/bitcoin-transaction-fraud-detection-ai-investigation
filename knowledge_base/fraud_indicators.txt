FRAUD INDICATORS

Fraud detection systems identify transactions that exhibit characteristics associated with illicit activity. A single indicator does not necessarily prove fraud. Multiple independent indicators provide stronger evidence.

MODEL-BASED RISK

A transaction receiving a high probability from a supervised machine learning model should be considered a high-risk candidate for investigation. Model probability represents the model's estimated likelihood of the illicit class based on learned patterns. It is not proof that the transaction is fraudulent.

MODEL CORROBORATION

When XGBoost and GraphSAGE both assign high fraud probabilities to the same transaction, the evidence is stronger than when only one model assigns high risk.

XGBoost primarily captures transaction-level feature patterns.

GraphSAGE incorporates information from connected transactions in the transaction graph.

Strong agreement between the models indicates that both transaction-level and graph-based patterns support the risk assessment.

MODEL DISAGREEMENT

When XGBoost assigns high risk while GraphSAGE assigns lower risk, the transaction may contain strong transaction-level patterns without strong supporting graph evidence.

When GraphSAGE assigns high risk while XGBoost assigns lower risk, the transaction may exhibit suspicious graph relationships even though its individual transaction features are less suspicious.

Model disagreement should trigger closer investigation rather than automatic rejection.

GRAPH INDICATORS

Suspicious graph behavior may include:

- Connections to multiple high-risk transactions.
- High concentration of risky neighbors.
- Strong connectivity to previously identified suspicious activity.
- Unusual local graph structure.
- Multiple transactions associated with suspicious transaction clusters.

A high-risk transaction with many high-risk neighbors provides stronger graph evidence than a high-risk transaction whose neighbors are mostly low-risk or unknown.

NEIGHBOR INTERPRETATION

A transaction's graph neighbors represent transactions connected through the transaction graph.

Unknown neighbors should not automatically be considered legitimate or fraudulent. Their lack of a known label means that their true class is unknown.

Risk scores assigned to neighbors can provide additional model-based evidence when available.

SHAP INDICATORS

SHAP values explain the contribution of individual features to an XGBoost prediction.

A positive SHAP value for the fraud class means that the feature pushes the model toward a higher fraud prediction.

A negative SHAP value means that the feature pushes the model toward a lower fraud prediction.

Large absolute SHAP values indicate stronger influence on the individual prediction.

SHAP explanations describe model behavior. They do not establish that a feature is inherently fraudulent.

IMPORTANT LIMITATION

Feature identifiers such as feature_52 or feature_89 are model input dimensions. Unless their original semantic meaning is known from the dataset documentation, an investigator should not assign a real-world interpretation to the feature.

The system should therefore state that a feature contributed strongly to the model prediction rather than inventing what that feature represents.
